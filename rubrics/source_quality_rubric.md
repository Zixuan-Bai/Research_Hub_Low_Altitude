# Source Quality Rubric

Use this rubric for academic and non-academic sources.

## 1. Source Type

- `academic_paper`
- `standard`
- `white_paper`
- `policy`
- `regulation`
- `deployed_system`
- `planned_system`
- `technical_report`
- `news`

## 2. Trust Level

### High

- IEEE / ACM Transactions or equivalent strong journals;
- Nature / Science family;
- top conferences recognized by the field;
- standards bodies;
- government or regulator documents;
- official documentation for deployed systems;
- measurement reports with transparent methodology.

### Medium

- peer-reviewed but not clearly top-tier papers;
- credible industry white papers with limited methodology;
- public technical reports;
- reputable news reporting on policy or deployments.

### Low

- weak or unclear venue;
- marketing-heavy white paper without technical detail;
- unsupported blog posts;
- single-paper claims with no corroboration;
- purely conceptual academic work with no practical validation.

## 3. Use in Route Cards

- High-trust sources can support assumptions, motivation, and baseline selection.
- Medium-trust sources can support open questions and candidate route framing.
- Low-trust sources can inspire ideas but cannot independently support a route decision.

## 4. Practicality Warning

If a topic is popular academically but lacks standards, deployed systems, industrial roadmaps, or credible policy demand, mark it as `deployment uncertain`.
